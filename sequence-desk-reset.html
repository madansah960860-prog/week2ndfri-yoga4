Sequence

Desk Reset

Fifteen steps done in and around one chair, with your shoes on and your clothes on. No mat, no floor, no getting changed.

Total 12 minutes Beginner Chair required Seated and standing
12Minutes total
15Steps
1Prop required

What you need

  • A sturdy chair with a flat seat and no arms. A dining chair is ideal. An office chair works only if it does not swivel or roll, so lock it or lean it against a wall first.
  • Roughly three feet by three feet of clear space around the chair.
  • Nothing else. This sequence is designed for the middle of a working day.

Chair practice is not a reduced version of floor practice. The chair supports part of your body weight, which lets you work more precisely in the joints you are actually trying to move. Plenty of long-time practitioners choose the chair for exactly that reason.

Before you practice This is general movement education, not medical advice. Stop if you feel pain, and talk with a qualified healthcare professional before starting a new physical practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, post-surgical or managing a health condition. Read the full Health & Safety Disclaimer.

Seated setup

Upaviṣṭha Tāḍāsana

0:45

Select any step below to jump to it. The player counts each hold down and moves to the next position automatically.

  1. 1. Seated setup
    Sit forward off the chair back. Both feet flat, knees over ankles, sitting bones even. Breathe in for four, out for six.     0:45
  2. 2. Seated Cat and Cow
    Hands on the knees. Exhale and round the spine, inhale and lift the chest. Ten slow rounds, moving from the pelvis.     1:00
  3. 3. Seated side bend, lengthening the left side
    Right hand on the seat beside you, left arm reaching up and over. Keep both sitting bones down.     0:45
  4. 4. Seated side bend, lengthening the right side
    Change sides. Reach out to the side rather than folding forward.     0:45
  5. 5. Seated twist, right
    Sit tall first, then turn to the right. Left hand on the right knee, right hand on the chair back. Turn from the ribs, and let the head come last.     0:45
  6. 6. Seated twist, left
    Change sides. Keep both feet flat and the knees level.     0:45
  7. 7. Seated figure four, right
    Cross the right ankle above the left knee, right knee falling out to the side. Sit tall, then hinge forward from the hips a few inches. Stop as soon as you feel the outer right hip.     1:00
  8. 8. Seated figure four, left
    Change sides. If the ankle does not reach the knee, rest the foot on the opposite shin instead.     1:00
  9. 9. Chair forward fold
    Feet wider than the hips. Fold forward between the knees, forearms on the thighs or hands toward the floor. Let the head hang. Come up slowly.     1:00
  10. 10. Standing chair hinge
    Stand behind the chair, hands on its back, walk the feet back and hinge at the hips until the arms and spine make one long line. Knees softly bent.     1:00
  11. 11. Right heel on the seat
    Place the right heel on the chair seat with the leg straight but not locked. Hinge forward from the hips with a long spine. Very small movement.     0:45
  12. 12. Left heel on the seat
    Change sides. Keep both hips facing forward.     0:45
  13. 13. Neck release, right
    Sit down. Tip the right ear toward the right shoulder. Let the left shoulder stay heavy. No hand pressure on the head.     0:30
  14. 14. Neck release, left
    Change sides. Move slowly through the middle.     0:30
  15. 15. Seated close
    Sit still. Four counts in, six counts out, six rounds. Then go back to whatever you were doing.     0:45

Beginner modification path

Use this version if you are new, if you are stiff after a long stretch of sitting, or if standing up and down repeatedly is uncomfortable.

  • Stay seated the whole way through. Replace step 10 with a seated chest opening: hold the sides of the seat, lift the chest and draw the shoulder blades toward each other for one minute. Replace steps 11 and 12 with a seated leg extension, straightening one leg forward with the heel on the floor and hinging slightly forward for 45 seconds per side.
  • Feet not reaching the floor. Put a book, a ream of paper or a folded jacket under them so the thighs are supported and the knees sit at about ninety degrees.
  • Chair too low or too soft. Sit on a folded towel or a firm cushion so the hips are slightly higher than the knees. This makes every seated shape easier to sit tall in.
  • Step 7 and 8, figure four. If the ankle does not cross above the knee comfortably, rest the foot on the opposite shin, or simply turn one knee out to the side with the foot still on the floor.
  • Step 9, forward fold. Stop with the forearms on the thighs. There is no requirement to reach the floor.
  • Steps 13 and 14, neck. Keep the movement very small. Never pull on the head with a hand.

When to use this one

The honest answer is at the point where you notice your shoulders have climbed toward your ears and you have been in the same position for two hours. That is usually somewhere between the middle of the morning and the middle of the afternoon.

Twelve minutes is long enough to work through everything that gets stiff from sitting and short enough to fit between meetings. If you have five minutes rather than twelve, do steps 2, 5, 6, 9 and 15.

If you want a fuller practice at the end of the day, the Evening Wind-Down picks up where this leaves off.
