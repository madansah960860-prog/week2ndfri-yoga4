Sequences

Four practices for the time you actually have.

Each one written out pose by pose, with a hold time for every shape, a beginner path through the whole thing, and a sequence player that counts it down for you.

How sequences work

Everything you need before you start

Each page carries a sequence player with a per-pose hold timer and a pause control, so you can follow along without watching a clock, plus a print stylesheet so you can put a paper copy next to your mat.

Sequences are designed to be repeated. Doing the same ten minutes four times in a week teaches you far more than doing four different practices once each.

These are general movement education, not medical advice. Read the Health & Safety Disclaimer before practicing.

All sequences

Pick your minutes

Ten to forty minutes, beginner to intermediate, no props to two blocks and a strap.

10-Minute Morning

10 minutesBeginnerNo props required

Six shapes that take the spine through flexion, extension, rotation and side bending before the day starts. Nothing here requires you to be warm already.

Open the sequence

Desk Reset

12 minutesBeginnerChair required

Done entirely in and around a chair with your shoes on. Eight positions for the hips, upper back, shoulders and neck after a long stretch of sitting.

Open the sequence

Evening Wind-Down

20 minutesBeginnerBolster or two pillows

Five supported shapes held long, all at floor level, all with a prop doing the work. The practice this site was designed around.

Open the sequence

Gentle Full Practice

40 minutesBeginner to intermediateTwo blocks and a strap

A complete arc: floor warm-up, standing work, seated shapes and a long rest. Nineteen shapes across twenty-nine timed steps, with a written chair and wall path through every stage.

Open the sequence
Before you pick one

How to choose

If you are torn between two, start with the shorter one and repeat it for a week before you add anything.

  • If you have never practiced before, start with the 10-Minute Morning and repeat it for a week before adding anything.
  • If you sit for work, the Desk Reset is the one that will actually get used, because it needs no mat, no floor and no change of clothes.
  • If you practice in the evening, the Evening Wind-Down is built for a slow twenty minutes with a bolster, whatever the light is doing outside.
  • If you have forty minutes and want a full practice, the Gentle Full Practice is the complete arc, and it has a written beginner path at every stage.
