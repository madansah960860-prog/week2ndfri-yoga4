Sequence

Evening Wind-Down

Seven long holds over twenty minutes, all at floor level, all with a prop taking your weight. Read as well after a long day as it does first thing.

Total 20 minutes Beginner Bolster or two pillows Floor and wall
20Minutes total
7Long holds
4Props used

What you need

  • One bolster, or two firm bed pillows placed end to end, or a couch cushion, or a rolled comforter tied with a belt. What matters is that it is firm enough not to flatten under your body weight.
  • Two folded blankets. One for under the head and one for under the knees or over you.
  • One block, or a thick book, for the twists.
  • A clear stretch of wall for the last long shape, wide enough for your shoulders.
  • Warm clothes or socks. You will stop generating heat about four minutes in.

Every shape in this sequence is passive. Once the props are set up, the muscles are meant to be able to switch off completely. If you are still working to stay in a position, add more support rather than pushing through it.

Before you practice This is general movement education, not medical advice. Stop if you feel pain, and talk with a qualified healthcare professional before starting a new physical practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, post-surgical or managing a health condition. From the second trimester of pregnancy onward, long periods lying flat on the back are generally not advised, so use the side-lying options in the modification path.   1. 1. Supported Child's Pose
    Kneel with the knees wide and a bolster lengthwise between the thighs. Rest the whole front of the torso along it and turn the head to one side. Change the head turn at ninety seconds. Full guide     3:00
  2. 2. Reclined twist, knees to the right
    Lie on your back, draw the knees in, then let them fall to the right onto a folded blanket or a block so they are supported rather than hanging. Left shoulder stays down.     2:30
  3. 3. Reclined twist, knees to the left
    Come back through center slowly and change sides. Move the head only if the neck is comfortable.     2:30
  4. 4. Supported Bridge on a bolster
    Lie back, lift the hips and slide the bolster crosswise under the bony back of the pelvis. Feet flat, hip width. Let everything switch off. Full guide     4:00
  5. 5. Supported Seated Forward Fold
    Sit up on a folded blanket, legs forward with knees bent over a rolled blanket. Lay the bolster across the thighs and rest the torso and forehead down on it. Full guide     3:00
  6. 6. Legs Up the Wall
    Sit sideways to the wall and swivel the legs up. Hips six to twelve inches away, blanket under the torso, arms open. Full guide     4:00
  7. 7. Close
    Bend the knees, roll to one side, then lie flat with a rolled blanket under the knees and a blanket over you. Four counts in, eight counts out.     1:00

Beginner modification path

Long supported holds are harder than they look, not physically but in terms of staying settled. This path shortens the holds and raises the support.

  • Halve every hold. Run the whole sequence at ninety seconds, seventy five seconds, seventy five, two minutes, ninety seconds, two minutes and one minute. That is a ten minute practice with the same shapes.
  • Step 1. If kneeling is uncomfortable, do this in a chair instead. Sit forward, feet flat, knees apart, and fold over a stack of cushions on your thighs.
  • Steps 2 and 3. Put more height under the knees. A bolster under them means the twist stays gentle and the opposite shoulder can stay down.
  • Step 4. Use a block on its lowest setting instead of a bolster if the bolster feels too high, or lie with the calves on a chair seat and skip the lift entirely.
  • Step 5. Add a second blanket on top of the bolster so the forehead has somewhere to land without any forward fold at all.
  • Step 6. Take the chair version instead. Lie on the floor with the lower legs resting on a chair seat, knees bent at ninety degrees. It is genuinely more comfortable for most people, and it is an equal option rather than a fallback.
  • Pregnancy path. Replace steps 2, 3, 4 and 6 with side-lying rest on the left side with a pillow between the knees and one under the head, and keep steps 1 and 5 with generous height so there is no pressure on the abdomen.

Setting up

Put the phone in another room, or face down and out of reach. If the floor is cold, practice on a rug or put a blanket under the mat. This works exactly the same at 7am on the kitchen floor as it does at 11pm in the bedroom — the page follows your device's light or dark setting automatically, so there is nothing to adjust before you start.

Twenty minutes of stillness is long. Expect the mind to be busy for the first shape and to settle somewhere around the second or third. That is the normal shape of it, not a sign that it is going badly.

The hold timer in the corner of this page quietly dims while each hold runs. It is a focus aid, not a mood setting — one less bright thing to look at while you are meant to be doing nothing. Switch it off in the timer panel if you would rather keep the screen at full brightness.
