Glossary

Sanskrit and English terms you will meet in a class or on this site, in plain language, with a rough guide to saying them.

A short note on Sanskrit

Sanskrit pose names are written here with diacritical marks, which are the small lines and dots above and below letters. They mark vowel length and specific consonants, and they are the reason Bālāsana and Balāsana are different words.

Three rules cover most of what you need:

  • A line above a vowel means hold it longer. The ā in āsana is the a in father. A plain a is closer to the u in cup.
  • Ś and ṣ are both roughly sh. So Śavāsana starts shah, and Paścimottānāsana has a sh in the middle.
  • A dot under a letter means the tongue curls back. English speakers can approximate it with an ordinary t, d or n and be understood.

Nobody in a studio will correct your pronunciation. Making the attempt is the respectful part.

Breath, practice and studio terms

Words you will hear in class
TermMeaning
prāṇāyāmaBreath practice. Deliberately changing the length, path or rhythm of the breath for a set period.
Nāḍī ŚodhanaAlternate nostril breathing. See Breathwork Basics.
UjjāyīA breath with a slight constriction at the throat, producing a soft ocean-like sound. Common in Vinyasa and Ashtanga.
Sama VṛttiEqual-length breathing, the basis of box breathing.
vinyāsaPlacing in a particular way. In class it usually means a specific short sequence linking one pose to the next.
dṛṣṭiA soft gaze point. Used to steady balance poses.
bandhaA lock or binding. In practice, a subtle engagement in the pelvic floor or abdomen.
mudrāA gesture, usually made with the hands.
oṃA syllable chanted at the start or end of some classes. Participation is always optional.
namasteA greeting. Used in many Western studios to close a class. Usage varies widely.
sthira sukhamSteady and comfortable. A phrase from the Yoga Sūtras often used to describe how a pose should feel.
HaṭhaOn a timetable, a slower class of individual poses. Styles explained.
YinLong, passive, floor-based holds of two to five minutes.
RestorativeFully supported shapes held for five to twenty minutes with no stretch intended.
MysoreA self-paced Ashtanga class where each student works through their own portion.
Primary SeriesThe first fixed sequence in Ashtanga.
asymmetryThe ordinary fact that your two sides differ. Not a fault to be corrected.
contraindicationA reason to avoid or modify a particular pose. Every pose page on this site lists them.
counter poseA shape taken after another to move the spine in the opposite direction.
hands-on adjustmentA teacher physically adjusting your position. You can always decline, and a good studio will ask first.
propAny object that changes the geometry of a pose. See Props & Modifications.
modificationA different version of a pose. An equal alternative, not a lesser one.
flexionBending, as when the spine rounds forward.
extensionStraightening or arching, as when the chest lifts in a backward bend.
rotationTurning around the long axis, as in a twist.
lateral flexionSide bending.
neutral spineThe spine held in its natural curves rather than flattened or exaggerated.
hip hingeFolding at the hip joints with a long spine, rather than rounding the back.
hyperextensionA joint pushed past straight, most often the knees or elbows. Worth avoiding.
