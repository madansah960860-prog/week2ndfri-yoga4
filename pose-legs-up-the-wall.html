Pose guide

Legs Up the Wall

Viparīta Karaṇī

Lying on the back with the legs resting vertically against a wall. Once you are in it, the pose requires nothing from you at all, which is the entire point.

Beginner Hold 5 to 15 minutes Legs, low back, whole body Wall and blanket

Pose breakdown

Viparīta meaning inverted or reversed, karaṇī meaning action or doing

Entry steps

  1. Clear a stretch of wall wide enough for your shoulders, and place a folded blanket on the floor where your torso will lie.
  2. Sit sideways on the blanket with your right hip and right shoulder touching the wall, knees bent in toward your chest.
  3. Lower onto your right elbow, then your right shoulder, and at the same time swing both legs up the wall as your torso turns to face the ceiling.
  4. The movement is one continuous swivel. It looks ungainly the first few times and then it becomes easy.
  5. Shuffle the hips closer to or further from the wall until the low back feels settled. Hips touching the wall is a stronger position. Six to twelve inches away is more comfortable for most people.
  6. Let the legs rest against the wall without holding them. Feet relaxed, knees softly bent if straight legs pull at the hamstrings.
  7. Rest the arms out to the sides at about forty five degrees, palms up.
  8. To come out, bend the knees, roll onto one side, rest there for thirty seconds, then press up to sitting.

Alignment cues

  • Distance from the wall is personal. Longer hamstrings can be closer. If the pelvis is tipping and the low back is arching, move further away.
  • Head level or slightly higher than the chest. A thin folded blanket under the head keeps the chin from tipping back.
  • Legs resting, not working. If the front of the thighs is engaged, the legs are being held rather than supported. Bend the knees a little.
  • Shoulders down away from the ears and the back of the ribcage broad on the floor.
  • Feet neutral. Do not point or flex hard. Let the ankles be soft.
  • Eyes closed or softly resting on a point on the ceiling. Cover the eyes with a folded cloth if the room is bright.

Breath pattern

  • Breathe in and out through the nose without steering it for the first minute.
  • Then, if you would like a pattern, take four counts in and six counts out for ten rounds and return to natural breathing.
  • Let the belly rise and fall freely. There is nothing pressing on it in this shape.
  • Use the breath pacer on our Breathwork Basics page if you like something to follow.

Suggested hold

  • Short version: 5 minutes.
  • Standard: 8 to 10 minutes.
  • Long: up to 15 minutes, with the eyes covered and a blanket over the body.
  • Come out if the feet start to tingle or go numb, which simply means it is time to move.

Common mistakes

  • Hips too close to the wall. Pressing the sitting bones against the wall when the hamstrings are not long enough tips the pelvis and arches the low back for ten minutes.
  • No blanket under the torso. A bare hard floor under the back of the ribs makes a ten minute hold uncomfortable for no reason.
  • Straight, locked legs. Locked knees create a constant low-grade pull. Let them bend slightly.
  • Getting in and out quickly. The swivel entry protects the low back. Rolling in feet first tends to twist it.
  • Getting cold. Body temperature drops when you stop moving. Cover yourself before you settle, not five minutes in.
  • Phone in reach. The pose is ten quiet minutes. That is what it is for.

Accessible modifications

  • Chair version. Lie on the floor with the lower legs resting on the seat of a chair, knees bent at ninety degrees. This gives the same rest for the legs and low back without needing the hamstring length that the wall version asks for. For many people this is the more comfortable pose, not a lesser one.
  • Bolster under the pelvis. Slide a bolster or a firmly folded blanket under the pelvis so the hips are a few inches higher than the chest. This adds a gentle lift and makes the shape feel more supported.
  • Sofa version. Lie on the floor with the calves resting on a sofa seat. Functionally identical to the chair version and available in most rooms.
  • Strap around the thighs. Loop a strap around the thighs at hip width so the legs can rest into it instead of being held together.
  • Bed version. Lie on a bed with the legs up the headboard or against the adjacent wall. Useful if getting down to the floor is difficult.
  • Knees bent against the wall. Rest the soles of the feet flat on the wall with the knees bent. All of the rest, none of the hamstring length required.

Progression

  • Bound Angle at the wall. Once settled, let the soles of the feet come together and the knees fall out to the sides, sliding the feet down the wall. Support the outer thighs with blocks or cushions. Hold 3 to 5 minutes.
  • Wide legs at the wall. Let the legs open out to the sides along the wall, keeping the knees soft. Hold 2 to 4 minutes and use the hands on the inner thighs to come back to center.
Approach with care Take extra care or choose the chair version if you have glaucoma or another eye condition where a healthcare professional has advised against inverting, if you have uncontrolled high blood pressure, a hernia, a neck injury, if you are in the second or third trimester of pregnancy where lying flat on the back for long periods is generally not advised, or if you experience reflux when lying flat. Stop if you feel pressure in the head, face or eyes, if the legs go numb rather than simply tingling, or if the low back becomes uncomfortable. Work with a qualified teacher for hands-on guidance. The best pose for the end of a day

This is the shape most likely to survive a bad week. It takes twenty seconds to set up, it needs no mat, and it works in a hotel room, a spare bedroom or the strip of floor beside a bed. If you only ever do one thing from this site, this is a reasonable candidate.

It closes the Evening Wind-Down and appears at the end of the Gentle Full Practice.

What to feel for

Nothing working, everything draining

Once you're settled, the legs should feel like they belong to someone else. No holding, no adjusting, no gentle flexing to check in. A light tingle as the legs unload is normal; numbness is not, and is the cue to come down. This is the pose to reach for when you cannot name what you need — it rarely disappoints.
