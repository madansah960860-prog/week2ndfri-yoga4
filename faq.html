Starting out

Ten minutes, three times a week, repeating the same sequence. That is enough to build the habit, and the repetition is what teaches you the shapes. Do our 10-Minute Morning three times in a week before you try anything else.

Longer sessions are not better in the first month. Consistent short ones are.

Props and equipment

Gear and substitutes

A mat is the only item on our list with no good household substitute for standing poses, because its actual job is stopping your hands and feet sliding. For floor-only practice, a large towel on carpet works. Hardwood without a mat is genuinely slippery, so keep early practices seated and lying down until you have one.

Two thick hardcover books taped shut, or two firmly folded bath towels, in place of blocks. A bathrobe belt, a long scarf or a dog leash in place of a strap. Two firm pillows in one pillowcase in place of a bolster. Our props guide lists a substitute for every item.

No. A prop changes the geometry of a pose so the intended action becomes available. A block under the hand in Triangle produces a structurally different and usually better pose, not an easier one. We wrote a whole guide about it: Props are not cheating.

Safety and sensation

What is normal, and what is not

Mild, general muscle soreness for a day or two after unfamiliar activity is ordinary. Sharp pain, pain at a joint line, numbness, tingling or anything that gets worse over the following days is not ordinary, and it is a reason to stop and speak with a qualified healthcare professional.

A useful stretch is broad, spread across the middle of a muscle, and something you could hold a conversation through. Stop if the sensation is sharp, pinching, burning, electrical, located at a joint line, or if it makes you hold your breath. Breath holding is the most reliable signal that you have gone further than your body currently wants to go.

That is a question for your healthcare provider, not for a website. What we can tell you is that every pose page here lists pregnancy-related contraindications where they apply, and that a teacher with specific prenatal training is the right person to work with. We are not qualified to advise on your individual situation and we will not try.

We do not answer that question, and you should be cautious of any site that does. Aruna Yoga Method publishes general movement education. We describe what a pose does physically, which muscles lengthen and which joints move. We make no claims about treating, relieving or preventing any condition. If you have a specific concern, that is a conversation for a qualified healthcare professional. See our Health & Safety Disclaimer.

Stop immediately for chest pain or pressure, shortness of breath at rest, dizziness or fainting, sudden severe headache, visual changes, numbness or weakness in a limb, or any sharp pain. In the United States, call 911 for a medical emergency.
