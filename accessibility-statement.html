Accessibility statement

What we have built in, what we know is imperfect, and how to tell us when something does not work.

Effective date: 09/01/2026. Last updated: 09/01/2026.

1. Our commitment

Aruna Yoga Method LLC aims to conform to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines version 2.1 at Level AA across this website. We consider accessibility part of building the site rather than something added afterward, and we treat a reported barrier as a defect to be fixed.

This commitment matters here for a specific reason. A site about moving your body should be usable by people whose bodies and senses work in different ways. Every pose guide includes chair, wall and prop-supported options as equal alternatives, and we take the same approach to the interface.

2. What we have built in

Structure and navigation

  • Semantic HTML5 landmarks on every page: header, nav, main and footer.
  • A skip-to-content link as the first focusable element on every page.
  • A single, logical heading hierarchy per page, starting at one h1.
  • The current page is marked in the navigation with aria-current.
  • Breadcrumb trails on interior pages.

Keyboard

  • Every interactive element is reachable and operable by keyboard alone, including the mobile menu, dropdowns, accordions, filters, the hold timer, the breath pacer and the sequence player.
  • Visible focus indicators are present on every focusable element and are never removed. We use a two pixel clay-coloured outline with a three pixel offset.
  • The Escape key closes open menus and returns focus to the control that opened them.
  • Dropdown menus are real buttons with aria-expanded, not hover-only regions.

Color and contrast

  • Body text is dark ink on warm paper by default, and inverts automatically to light ink on a near-black ground when your system is set to a dark theme. Both pairings are built to sit comfortably above the 4.5:1 minimum for body text.
  • Our clay accent color, used for links, eyebrows and active states, is chosen to clear the same 4.5:1 minimum in both the light and dark palette.
  • Secondary and tertiary text colors are chosen to stay at or above 4.5:1, including placeholder text in forms.
  • Color is never the only way information is conveyed. Filter states use aria-pressed as well as color, and form errors use text as well as a border color.

Motion

  • All motion respects the prefers-reduced-motion setting. Under that setting, transitions and animations are reduced to near-instant, the clay-coloured glow on the hold timer is switched off, the breath pacer holds still, and scroll reveals are disabled entirely.
  • Content is never hidden behind an animation. Reveal effects enhance content that is already present in the markup, so the page reads correctly with JavaScript disabled or in a text-only reader.
  • Nothing on this site flashes, blinks or moves automatically in a way that could trigger photosensitive reactions.
  • The hold timer's page dimming can be switched off with a checkbox in the timer panel.

Forms

  • Every input has a visible label. We never use placeholder text as a label.
  • Errors appear inline, immediately below the field they relate to, in text.
  • Error messages are announced to assistive technology and the first field with an error receives focus on submit.
  • Inputs use aria-invalid and aria-describedby to connect fields with their hints and errors.
  • Autocomplete attributes are set so browsers and password managers can fill fields correctly.

Images and media

  • Every image carries descriptive alternative text. Decorative elements are marked aria-hidden.
  • Every image has width and height attributes so nothing shifts as the page loads.
  • There is no video and no audio on this site, so there is nothing here requiring captions or transcripts.
  • There is no autoplaying media of any kind.

Layout and zoom

  • The site is responsive from 320 pixels upward and is tested at 360, 768, 1024, 1440 and 1920 pixels.
  • Pinch zoom is not disabled. Content reflows to 400 percent zoom without horizontal scrolling.
  • Text uses relative units so browser text-size settings are respected.
  • Wide content such as tables scrolls inside its own container rather than forcing the page sideways.

Timing

  • Nothing on this site has a time limit imposed on the user.
  • The hold timer and the sequence player are entirely optional, can be paused at any point, and never remove content or advance the page.
  • Every sequence is written out in full as text, so the timers are a convenience and never the only route to the information.

3. Known limitations

We would rather list these than claim perfection.

  • Third-party advertising. Ad creatives are supplied by advertising partners and we do not control their internal accessibility. We reserve labeled, bordered space for them, keep them away from navigation and buttons, and block creatives reported as problematic. Membership removes advertising entirely.
  • Sanskrit pronunciation. Our glossary gives approximate English-language pronunciation in text. We do not currently offer audio pronunciation, which would be more useful for some readers. This is on our list.
  • Diacritical marks. Some screen readers pronounce Sanskrit diacritics unpredictably. We include the English pose name alongside every Sanskrit name for this reason.
  • Pose photography. Our alternative text describes the scene rather than teaching the pose. The written instructions carry the teaching, which is deliberate, but it means the images are less informative than they could be for someone who cannot see them.

4. How we test

  • Keyboard-only walkthrough of every page template.
  • Automated checks with browser accessibility tooling on every template.
  • Manual contrast measurement of every text and background color pairing in the design system.
  • Testing at the five breakpoints listed above and at 400 percent zoom.
  • An independent accessibility audit is planned before launch, and its date and findings will be published on this page.

5. Tell us when something does not work

If any part of this site creates a barrier for you, please tell us. Include the page address, what you were trying to do, and the browser and assistive technology you were using if you know it.

6. Formal approach

This statement reflects our own assessment against WCAG 2.1 Level AA. If you are not satisfied with our response to a report, you may escalate it by writing to the mailing address above marked for the attention of the accessibility lead. We take Title III of the Americans with Disabilities Act and Section 508 standards seriously as a baseline for what a public-facing site should do.
