Sequence

10-Minute Morning

Eleven steps that take the spine through every direction it moves, designed to be done cold, straight out of bed, without warming up first.

Total 10 minutes Beginner No props required Floor based
10Minutes total
11Steps
0Props required

What you need

  • A mat, or a rug, or a carpeted floor. A hard bare floor works if you have a folded blanket for the knees.
  • Optional: one folded blanket to pad the knees in the kneeling shapes.
  • Optional: a clear wall if you prefer Wall Dog to the floor version.
  • Roughly six and a half feet by three feet of clear floor.
Before you practice This is general movement education, not medical advice. Stop if you feel pain, and talk with a qualified healthcare professional before starting a new physical practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, post-surgical or managing a health condition.     Knees bent enough that the belly rests on the thighs. Let the head hang. Roll up slowly.     0:45

Beginner modification path

Follow this version for your first two or three weeks. It is the same sequence and the same ten minutes, with the load lowered at every step. It is not a shorter or lesser practice.

  • Steps 1 to 4, the kneeling shapes. Put a folded blanket under the knees every time, and a second one behind the knee joints in Child's Pose if the knees feel compressed. Rest your forehead on stacked fists or a block rather than the floor.
  • Step 5, Downward-Facing Dog. Take Wall Dog instead. Hands on the wall at hip height, feet walked back, spine long. Same shoulder and spine action, none of the wrist load.
  • Steps 6 and 7, Low Lunge. Keep both hands on a chair seat placed beside you rather than reaching for the floor. Take the back knee down on a doubled blanket.
  • Step 8, Standing Forward Fold. Rest the hands on a chair seat or two tall blocks. Bend the knees as much as you like.
  • Step 9, Mountain Pose. Stand with your back near a wall so you can touch it if you sway. Keep the arms at shoulder height instead of overhead if reaching up is uncomfortable.
  • Steps 10 and 11, Seated Twist. Do this sitting on a chair instead. Feet flat on the floor, hands on the chair back, turning from the ribs. The chair version is more precise than the floor version for most people.

If ten minutes is too much on a given morning, do steps 1, 2, 5 and 8 and finish. Four minutes done beats ten minutes skipped.

Why these poses, in this order

The sequence starts on the floor because the body has just spent seven or eight hours horizontal and asking it to stand and balance immediately is not the kindest opening. Child's Pose and Cat and Cow move the spine gently in flexion and extension while the joints are still stiff.

Thread the Needle adds rotation to the upper spine, which is the direction most often skipped. Downward-Facing Dog is the first shape that loads the arms, and it comes after the shoulders have been moving for three minutes rather than before.

Low Lunge lengthens the front of the hip, which is the tissue that spent the night in a shortened position. Standing Forward Fold and Mountain Pose bring you upright. The closing twists move the spine in its last remaining direction and settle everything down before you get on with the day.

Ten minutes is short enough to repeat daily, which is what makes it work.
