Pose library

Thirty-four poses, done properly.

English and Sanskrit names, filterable by difficulty, by the part of the body being worked, or by the prop that makes the shape available to you today.

How it's organized

Six poses have full written guides — entry steps, alignment cues, breath pattern, hold time, common mistakes, modifications, a progression and contraindication notes. Every other entry carries a short breakdown you can practice from right now, and full guides are added each month.

Every pose on this site is general movement education, not medical advice. Please read the Health & Safety Disclaimer and work with a qualified teacher for hands-on guidance.

Showing all 34 poses

  • Child's Pose Bālāsana Beginner / Spine, hips / Blanket optional / Full guide Kneeling and folding forward over the thighs. Lengthens the back body and gives the head somewhere to rest. The shape to return to at any point in any practice.
  • Downward-Facing Dog Adho Mukha Śvānāsana Beginner / Shoulders, hamstrings, spine / Blocks or wall / Full guide An inverted V with hands and feet on the floor. Loads the shoulders and lengthens the back of the legs. Frequently taught before the wrists are ready for it.
  • Warrior II Vīrabhadrāsana II Beginner / Hips, legs / No prop needed / Full guide A wide stance with the front knee bent, hips open to the side and arms level with the floor. Builds standing endurance in the thighs and hips.
  • Supported Bridge Setu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana Beginner / Spine, hips / Block / Full guide Lying on the back with the hips lifted onto a block. A passive backward bend that asks nothing of the legs once it is set up.
  • Seated Forward Fold Paścimottānāsana Beginner / Hamstrings, spine / Strap and blanket / Full guide Sitting with the legs straight and folding forward from the hip joints. The pose where most people learn to stop chasing their toes.
  • Legs Up the Wall Viparīta Karaṇī Beginner / Legs, spine / Wall / Full guide Lying on the back with the legs resting vertically against a wall. A long, quiet shape that requires no effort to hold.
  • Mountain Pose Tāḍāsana Beginner / Whole body / No prop needed Standing with the feet hip width, weight even across both feet, arms alongside the body. Hold 30 to 60 seconds. This is where you set the reference points every standing pose returns to: feet grounded, knees soft rather than locked, ribs stacked over the pelvis, crown lifting. Stand with your back against a wall to feel where your body actually is against a straight line.
  • Cat and Cow Marjaryāsana and Bitilāsana Beginner / Spine / Blanket under knees On hands and knees, rounding the spine on an exhale and arching it on an inhale. Move for 8 to 12 rounds, one movement per breath. Keep the elbows soft and let the movement travel from the tailbone up rather than starting at the neck. A folded blanket under the knees makes this comfortable on hard floors.
  • Table Top Bharmanāsana Beginner / Spine, shoulders / Blanket under knees Hands under shoulders, knees under hips, spine long. Hold 30 to 45 seconds. The base position for a large part of floor practice. Press the floor away so the space between the shoulder blades stays broad rather than sinking.
  • Cobra Bhujaṅgāsana Beginner / Spine, chest / No prop needed Lying face down, hands under the shoulders, lifting the chest with very little arm push. Hold 15 to 30 seconds, repeat twice. Keep the elbows bent and drawn toward the ribs. If the low back complains, lower and take Sphinx instead.
  • Sphinx Sālamba Bhujaṅgāsana Beginner / Spine / Bolster optional Lying face down on the forearms, elbows under or slightly ahead of the shoulders. Hold 1 to 3 minutes. The most sustainable gentle backward bend because the forearms carry the weight. Slide the elbows further forward to reduce the curve.
  • Locust Śalabhāsana Intermediate / Back, legs / Blanket under hips Lying face down, lifting the chest, arms and legs away from the floor. Hold 15 to 25 seconds, repeat two or three times. This is muscular work for the back of the body rather than a stretch. Lift the legs only, then the chest only, if lifting both at once is too much.
  • Low Lunge Añjaneyāsana Beginner / Hips, front of thigh / Blanket and blocks One foot forward, back knee down, hips settling toward the floor. Hold 45 to 90 seconds per side. Pad the back knee with a folded blanket every time. Blocks under the hands let you keep the chest lifted without collapsing into the front hip.
  • High Lunge Aśva Sañcalanāsana Intermediate / Legs, hips / Blocks optional A long stance with the back knee lifted and the front knee over the ankle. Hold 20 to 40 seconds per side. Demanding for the back leg. Lower the back knee at any point and it becomes Low Lunge, which is not a lesser pose.
  • Warrior I Vīrabhadrāsana I Intermediate / Hips, shoulders / No prop needed Front knee bent, back foot turned in around 45 degrees, arms overhead. Hold 20 to 40 seconds per side. Take the feet wider apart from side to side than feels natural. Narrow feet are the reason this pose feels unstable for most people.
  • Triangle Utthita Trikoṇāsana Intermediate / Side body, hamstrings / Block A long straight-legged side bend with the lower hand on a block. Hold 30 to 45 seconds per side. Put the block on its tallest setting first. Reaching for the floor is what turns this pose into a collapse through the front ribs.
  • Extended Side Angle Utthita Pārśvakoṇāsana Intermediate / Side body, hips / Block From Warrior II, the forearm rests on the front thigh or the hand comes to a block outside the front foot. Hold 30 to 45 seconds per side. Keep the top shoulder rolling back so the chest stays turned toward the ceiling.
  • Chair Pose Utkaṭāsana Beginner / Legs / Wall or chair option Knees bent, hips back, arms reaching forward or up. Hold 20 to 30 seconds. Sit back far enough to see your toes past your knees. Practice it with your back sliding down a wall to learn the angle without loading the knees.
  • Tree Pose Vṛkṣāsana Beginner / Balance, hips / Wall Standing on one leg with the other foot on the ankle, calf or inner thigh. Hold 30 to 45 seconds per side. Never place the foot against the side of the knee. Stand with your shoulder near a wall so you can touch it whenever you wobble.
  • Warrior III Vīrabhadrāsana III Advanced / Balance, legs / Blocks or wall Balancing on one leg with the torso and back leg roughly parallel to the floor. Hold 15 to 25 seconds per side. Learn it with the hands on a wall at hip height first, which removes the balance element entirely and lets you find the shape.
  • Half Moon Ardha Candrāsana Advanced / Balance, hips / Block and wall Standing on one leg with the other lifted to hip height, lower hand on a tall block. Hold 20 to 30 seconds per side. Practice with your back lightly against a wall. The wall gives the top hip somewhere to stack against.
  • Standing Forward Fold Uttānāsana Beginner / Hamstrings, spine / Blocks Folding forward from standing with the knees generously bent. Hold 30 to 60 seconds. Bend the knees as much as you need to let the belly rest on the thighs. Straight legs are not the goal and never were.
  • Wide-Legged Forward Fold Prasārita Pādottānāsana Beginner / Hamstrings, inner legs / Block Feet wide and parallel, folding forward with the hands on the floor or a block. Hold 45 to 90 seconds. Bring the block to the tallest setting so the spine can stay long. Turn the toes very slightly in to keep the hips level.
  • Pyramid Pose Pārśvottānāsana Intermediate / Hamstrings / Two blocks A short straight-legged stance folding over the front leg. Hold 30 to 45 seconds per side. Blocks on either side of the front foot are not optional for most people. Shorten the stance until the hips can stay square to the front.
  • Bound Angle Baddha Koṇāsana Beginner / Hips / Blocks and blanket Sitting with the soles of the feet together and the knees falling open. Hold 1 to 3 minutes. Sit on the edge of a folded blanket so the pelvis can tip forward, and rest a block under each thigh so the hips can release rather than hang.
  • Wide-Angle Seated Fold Upaviṣṭha Koṇāsana Intermediate / Hips, hamstrings / Bolster Sitting with the legs wide and folding forward from the hips. Hold 1 to 3 minutes. Place a bolster or a firm stack of cushions between the legs and rest the forehead and forearms on it. Take the legs less wide than you think.
  • Head-to-Knee Fold Jānu Śīrṣāsana Beginner / Hamstrings, side body / Strap One leg straight, the other foot to the inner thigh, folding over the straight leg. Hold 1 to 2 minutes per side. Loop a strap around the extended foot rather than reaching. Turn the chest toward the straight leg before you fold.
  • Half Lord of the Fishes Ardha Matsyendrāsana Intermediate / Spine / Blanket A seated twist with one foot crossed over the opposite thigh. Hold 45 to 60 seconds per side. Sit up on a folded blanket so the twist starts from a level pelvis. Turn from the base of the ribs, not from the neck.
  • Reclined Twist Supta Matsyendrāsana Beginner / Spine, hips / Blanket or bolster Lying on the back with the knees dropping to one side. Hold 1 to 3 minutes per side. Support the knees on a bolster or folded blanket so the shoulder on the opposite side can stay down. Let the head turn only if the neck is comfortable.
  • Pigeon Preparation Eka Pāda Rājakapotāsana, preparation Intermediate / Hips / Bolster and blanket Front shin angled across the mat, back leg extended behind. Hold 1 to 3 minutes per side. Support the front hip generously with a folded blanket so the pelvis stays level. If the front knee registers anything at all, take Reclined Figure Four instead.
  • Happy Baby Ānanda Bālāsana Beginner / Hips / Strap optional On the back, knees drawn toward the armpits, hands holding the outer feet. Hold 45 to 90 seconds. Loop a strap around each foot if the hands do not reach. Keep the head and shoulders resting on the floor rather than curling up.
  • Cow Face Arms Gomukhāsana, arm position Beginner / Shoulders / Strap One arm reaching up and bending behind the head, the other reaching behind the low back. Hold 30 to 45 seconds per side. Hold a strap between the hands. Hands rarely meet, and forcing them together is where shoulders get irritated.
  • Thread the Needle Pārśva Bālāsana Beginner / Shoulders, upper spine / Blanket From hands and knees, sliding one arm under the body and resting on the shoulder and side of the head. Hold 45 to 90 seconds per side. Keep the hips stacked over the knees. Pad the knees and rest the head on a folded blanket if the floor is too far.
  • Corpse Pose Śavāsana Beginner / Whole body / Bolster and blanket Lying flat on the back, arms a little away from the body, resting. Stay 5 to 10 minutes. Put a bolster or rolled blanket under the knees to take pressure off the low back, and cover yourself, because body temperature drops when you stop moving.
Before you begin

How to use this library

Hold times listed here are starting points, not targets. Use the hold timer in the corner of this page to run them, and stop earlier whenever the sensation changes character.

If you are new, start with the beginner filter and the no-prop filter, and build a short practice from four or five shapes. If you have blocks and a strap, switch the prop filter on and you will find that several intermediate shapes become straightforward.
