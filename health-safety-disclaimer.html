Health and safety disclaimer

Read this before you practice anything described on this site. It is short and it matters.

Effective date: 09/01/2026. Last updated: 09/01/2026.

The essential points

This website provides general movement education only. It is not medical advice, diagnosis, treatment or therapy. Consult a qualified healthcare professional before beginning any new physical practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, post-surgical, or managing a health condition. If you feel pain, stop.

1. This is education, not medicine

Aruna Yoga Method LLC publishes written descriptions of physical postures, sequences and breathing practices. That is the whole of what we do.

Nothing on this site is intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease, condition, injury or symptom. We do not make health, medical or therapeutic claims about any pose, sequence or breathing technique, and we will not tell you that a practice helps with back pain, anxiety, depression, insomnia, digestion, blood pressure, fertility, immunity or any other health matter. When we describe a pose we describe what happens physically: which muscles lengthen, which joints move, which parts of the body carry weight.

If you have a health question, that question belongs with a qualified healthcare professional who can assess you in person. We cannot and will not answer individual health questions by email, in our contact form or anywhere else.

2. No professional relationship

Reading this site does not create a doctor-patient, physical therapist-client, or teacher-student relationship between you and Aruna Yoga Method LLC or anyone who writes for it.

We do not present ourselves as a medical, physical therapy or clinical authority, because we are not one. Any teaching credential shown anywhere on this site is a placeholder for a real, verifiable qualification and should be treated as such until we publish verified details.

3. Talk to a healthcare professional first

Speak with a qualified healthcare professional before beginning any new physical practice. This is particularly important if any of the following apply to you:

  • You are pregnant, have recently given birth, or are trying to conceive.
  • You have had surgery in the past twelve months, or any surgery affecting the spine, abdomen, hips, shoulders or eyes.
  • You have a current or recent injury, or pain that has not been assessed.
  • You have a cardiovascular condition, high or low blood pressure, or a history of stroke.
  • You have a respiratory condition such as asthma.
  • You have osteoporosis, osteopenia, a disc condition, spinal stenosis, or any diagnosed joint condition.
  • You have glaucoma, retinal detachment or another eye condition.
  • You have a hernia, or any condition affected by pressure in the abdomen.
  • You have a neurological condition, a seizure disorder, or a history of fainting or dizziness.
  • You are taking medication that affects balance, blood pressure, heart rate or alertness.
  • You have a history of panic, dissociation or trauma that may be affected by breath practice or long stillness.

This list is not exhaustive. If you are unsure, ask.

4. Stop if you feel pain

Stop what you are doing and come out of the shape if you experience any of the following:

  • Sharp, stabbing, burning, pinching or electrical sensation.
  • Pain located at a joint line rather than in the middle of a muscle.
  • Numbness, tingling or weakness in a limb.
  • Dizziness, lightheadedness or feeling faint.
  • Chest pain or pressure, or shortness of breath at rest.
  • Sudden severe headache, or any change in vision.
  • Nausea.
  • Any need to hold your breath in order to stay in a position.

In the United States, call 911 for a medical emergency. If symptoms persist after you stop, contact a qualified healthcare professional.

5. Practicing from written instructions has limits

Written instructions cannot see you. They cannot notice your knee drifting inward, cannot tell that you are compensating on one side, and cannot assess whether a shape is appropriate for your body today.

Work with a qualified teacher for hands-on guidance, especially if you are new, returning from an injury, pregnant or postpartum, or working around any physical limitation. Our Find a Teacher page explains how to choose one and what to ask. Note that we do not verify the credentials of anyone in that directory and inclusion is not an endorsement.

6. Contraindications on every pose

Every pose guide on this site carries a short contraindication note listing who should approach that shape with care or avoid it, and instructing readers to work with a qualified teacher. Those notes are a starting point drawn from common teaching practice. They are not a substitute for individual assessment, and they cannot cover every circumstance.

Read the contraindication note before you practice the pose, not after.

7. What we will never publish

  • Claims that a pose, sequence or breathing technique treats, cures, heals, relieves or prevents any condition.
  • Weight-loss framing of any kind: no calorie or fat-burning figures, no body-transformation or before-and-after content, no toning or slimming claims, no commentary on body shape.
  • Dietary restriction, cleanse, fasting or detox content, or any pairing of practice with an eating plan.
  • Invented testimonials, reviews, ratings, credentials or qualifications.
  • Advice presented as clinical guidance.

8. Your responsibility

By using the material on this site you accept that you are choosing to participate in physical activity voluntarily, that you are responsible for judging whether any given practice suits your body, and that you assume the risks involved, to the fullest extent permitted by law. See section 3 of our Terms of Use for the full assumption of risk provision and section 12 for the limitation of liability.

9. Third-party content

Advertising, affiliate links and teacher directory listings on this site are not health advice and are not endorsed by us as such. Products advertised here are not vetted by us for any health purpose. See our Advertising & Affiliate Disclosure.

10. Contact
