Sequence

Gentle Full Practice

Nineteen shapes across twenty-nine timed steps. A complete arc from the floor to standing and back down again, with a written chair and wall path at every stage.

Total 40 minutes Beginner to intermediate Two blocks and a strap Floor, standing and wall
40Minutes total
29Timed steps
5Props used

What you need

  • A mat, or a non-slip rug. The standing section needs grip.
  • Two blocks, or two thick hardcover books of equal height.
  • A strap, or a bathrobe belt, a long scarf or a dog leash.
  • Two folded blankets, one to sit on and one for under the knees and head.
  • A clear stretch of wall for the closing shape, and a sturdy chair if you are using the modification path.
  • Roughly seven feet by four feet of clear floor for the standing section.
Before you practice This is general movement education, not medical advice. Stop if you feel pain, and talk with a qualified healthcare professional before starting a new physical practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, post-surgical or managing a health condition.     Lie on your back, knees bent or a blanket under them. Breathe in for four, out for six. Do not skip this.    1:30

  8. 8. Low Lunge, left foot forward

    Walk the hands back to the feet and stand up slowly. Knees bent, head heavy.    1:00
  10. 10. Mountain Pose

  11. 11. Warrior II, right foot forward

  12. 12. Warrior II, left foot forward
    Change sides.    0:40

  14. 14. Triangle, left
    Change sides.    0:45

Beginner modification path

This path keeps all twenty-nine steps and the same forty minutes. It uses a chair and a wall throughout. It is a complete practice in its own right, not a warm-up for the other one.

  • Steps 2 to 5, the floor warm-up. Do these seated on a chair. Seated Cat and Cow with the hands on the knees, seated twists in place of Thread the Needle, and a chair forward fold in place of Child's Pose.
  • Step 6, Downward-Facing Dog. Wall Dog. Hands on the wall at hip height, feet walked back, spine long.
  • Steps 7 and 8, Low Lunge. Stand facing a chair, place one foot on its seat with the knee bent, and shift weight forward gently. Or take the floor version with both hands on a chair beside you.
  • Steps 9 and 18, Standing Forward Fold. Hands on the chair seat, hinging at the hips with a long spine rather than folding all the way down.
  • Steps 11 and 12, Warrior II. Sit sideways on the chair with the front thigh supported along the seat and the back leg extended out to the side. Full upper body and hip action with the chair taking the leg work.
  • Steps 13 and 14, Triangle. Stand with your back against a wall and rest the lower hand on the chair seat instead of a block.
  • Steps 15 and 16, Tree Pose. Stand with one shoulder near a wall, foot at the ankle rather than the thigh, fingertips on the wall.
  • Step 17, Chair Pose. Stand in front of the chair and lower toward the seat, touching down and standing back up, five slow times.
  • Steps 19 to 24, seated work. Sit up higher on two folded blankets, or do all of these in a chair. The chair versions of the forward fold and the twist are more precise than their floor equivalents for most bodies.
  • Step 25, Supported Bridge. Lie with the calves on the chair seat instead.
  • Step 28, Legs Up the Wall. Calves on the chair seat, knees bent at ninety degrees.

Mixing the two paths is expected. Most people end up with a floor version of some steps and a chair version of others, and that is what a personal practice looks like.

How the arc is built

The practice opens on the floor with ninety seconds of nothing, which is there so that you arrive rather than start. The warm-up then moves the spine in flexion, extension and rotation before anything asks for strength.

The standing section is the middle third and the most physically demanding. It runs from lunges through the two wide standing shapes, then to balance, then to one short strength shape, and ends with a fold so the legs can recover.

Seated work follows because the body is warm by then and the hips and hamstrings will meet the shapes differently than they would at minute three. The last third is entirely supported, and the final thirteen minutes ask nothing of you at all.

If forty minutes is more than you have, run steps 1 to 10 and then jump to 25 and 29. That is an eighteen minute practice with a complete beginning, middle and end.
